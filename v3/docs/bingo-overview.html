Project BINGO

One trust layer, proven across five domains

A small kernel of cryptographic primitives that lets value move between parties who don't trust each other — and pays the people who actually created it. The same engine runs a print farm, a Wagyu ranch, a car hauler, and a promo coin.

18/18 test suites green · all stdlib · every claim independently verifiable

The problem isn't that we lack AI or automation — it's that the systems moving value run on trust-by-paperwork: a label, an invoice, a promise. BINGO replaces the paperwork with proof. Provenance you can check, payment that routes automatically to the maker, and fraud that can't settle instead of fraud you have to chase. Built not for profit extraction but so a skilled person — a designer, a rancher, a driver — meaningfully shares in what their work produces.

The shared kernel — everything below is built from these

Content-addressed assetsAn asset's ID is the hash of its content, so identity can't be separated from the thing — an STL, a provenance passport, a coin.
Ed25519 signaturesPure-Python, RFC 8032. Anyone holding a public key can verify — no secret, no server, no trust in the signer.
Hash-chained event logsEach event signed and linked to the last. Tamper, reorder, or forge one link and the chain breaks. This is the spine of every record.
Atomic value routingA payment splits across everyone who made the value, to the cent, in one transaction — royalty enforced at the point of the transaction.
Single-use ledgersA claim can be spent exactly once; a copy is caught by replay, not by trust. Survives restarts.
Independent verificationEvery record verifies from the document alone, offline. A skeptic checks it in seconds without believing a word you say.

The range is the argument: a bracket, a ribeye, a Porsche, and a coin have nothing in common except that each is a thing of value changing hands. That one engine handles all four is the evidence it's infrastructure, not a niche app.

The five verticals

Manufacturing / creator economy

designs → prints

A designer's file gets downloaded and printed a thousand times; they see none of it.

The design is a content-addressed asset with a royalty split. Every fabrication emits a signed proof-of-fabrication chain, and payment routes to the creator at the moment of the print — plus network-maintained reputation and grade-based acceptance.

Proven: a real part fabricated on a Creality K2, 44 signed PoF events, settled atomically ($0.40 royalty / $1.48 shop / $5.50 logistics). A creator opens one page and sees what every print paid them.

RWA provenance

premium physical goods

A5 Wagyu sells for $65/lb because of its provenance — genetics, feed, grade — yet "Wagyu" is among the most counterfeited words in food. The premium rests on a paper label.

Each cut carries a provenance passport: lineage, husbandry, harvest/grade, cold-chain custody — every link signed by the party who made it. The passport is the asset's content, so its ID binds to the exact chain of custody.

Proven: an A5 ribeye passport that verifies offline, with the third-generation rancher paid $12.87 on a single cut — routed automatically through the sale, not thanked in the marketing.

Tokenization

claims on real-world assets

Tokenizing physical goods (Brazil already does it with cattle) is only as honest as what backs the token. Usually: nothing checkable.

A token is a transferable claim pinned to a verified provenance passport — you can't mint a claim on a thing you can't prove. Ownership is a signed ledger; primary sales route proceeds through the provenance split, and resales carry a royalty back to it. Redemption requires a co-signed physical handoff.

Proven: 100 shares issued, traded, and redeemed against the A5 lot; the rancher earns $14.43 from token activity on top of the physical sale. Double-spend, forged signer, and fabricated payouts all caught on replay.

Auto transport

vehicle logistics

Double brokering — a load secretly re-brokered to an unaccountable carrier — costs the industry an estimated $700M–$1B/year. Every existing tool is detection.

The carrier's identity is cryptographically bound at booking. Pickup and delivery must be signed by that same identity, the customer co-signs condition at delivery, and escrow releases only to the bound carrier. A re-brokered load can't verify, so it can't settle.

Proven: a live demo where a different truck delivering flips the record to REJECTED and escrow BLOCKED — the fraud isn't caught, it simply can't get paid. New damage at delivery becomes an undeniable, both-ends-signed claim.

DGD promo coins

bearer instrument

A $25-redeemable QR coin is money you can photocopy — it needs to resist both counterfeiting and duplication.

The QR is an Ed25519-signed credential (counterfeit fails offline). A scratch-off code, committed by hash and hidden under a tamper-evident panel, means a photographed QR can't redeem. A persistent single-use ledger blocks every replay. A clean backend seam posts $25 USDC-of-DGD to the receiver's account.

Proven: a coin redeems $25 exactly once; counterfeit, photographed QR, and reused code all blocked — and the spent state survives a server restart, so it can't be double-redeemed after a crash.
